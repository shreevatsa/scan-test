    translated_stanza 101 (open start) [u2]
      r2: rendered. Good luck, the result of ( lit stored by ) past religious performances fructifies for man seasonably, as do trees.
    translated_stanza 102 [u3]
      r3: 102. Meritorious deeds done in a former life protect a man in a forest, on a battlefield, in the midst of foes, or water or fire, in the vast ocean, or on a mountain-top, whether he be asleep or careless or in a perilous condition.
    translated_stanza 103 [u4]
      r4: 103. O good man, to obtain your cherished desire, adore that goddess—Good conduct—that changes wicked men into good, fools into wise men, enemies into friends, the invisible into the visible and deadly poison into nectar, in an instant. Fix not your hopes on numerous other qualities with persistent application.
    translated_stanza 104 [u5]
      r5: 104. When good luck is favourable to one ( lit. closely linked ), a splendid mansion, sportive women, and prosperity brilliant with the white umbrella—all these things are enjoyed for a long time; but when unfavourable ( lit. when its thread is snapped ) behold ! all these vanish in all directions like a wreath of pearls, the thread of which is broken in the sport of amorous quarrels.
    translated_stanza 105 [u6]
      r6: 105. A wise man, bent on doing a thing, good or bad, should first carefully consider its consequence. The issue of actions done in hot haste becomes, till death, like a dart, poignant to the heart.
    translated_stanza 106 [u7]
      r7: 106. The luckless man that does not practise penance on coming into this Land of karman, cooks ( as it were ) with sandal-wood &c. for fuel, in a utensil made of the  Vaidûrya gem, the oil-cake of sesamum; or ploughs the earth with a golden ploughshare for the sake of the root of the arka tree, or makes a hedge round a crop of kodravas of camphor-trees having cut them down.

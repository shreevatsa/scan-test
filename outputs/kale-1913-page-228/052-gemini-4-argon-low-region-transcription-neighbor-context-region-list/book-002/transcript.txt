    stanza_note 40 'Sl. 40.' [u2]
      r2: Sl. 40. भङ्गुरवृत्तयः—evanescent or transient by nature. तैरेव चायं भवः—And wordly life ( is formed ) of them only. कृतम्— enough with. समाधीयताम्— fix in one place. कामोच्छित्तिवशे—which is (now) under control on account of desires being uprooted. स्वधामनि—in its own place or abode, viz. स्वयंप्रकाशे आत्मनि.
    stanza_note 41 'Sl. 41.' [u3]
      r3: Sl. 41. तृणत्व०—of as little consequence as a small blade of grass. यस्मिन्महाशासने—V. l. Some such words as लब्धे or अधिगते सति should be taken as understood after this locative. महाशासने—&c.—The knowledge of Brahma as expounded in the Upanishads; the highest truth taught by them ( the four Mahávákyas especially ); Ràmarshi explains this by परमात्मज्ञाने ( स्फुरिते सति ). Mr. Telang says—“It means whose commands are great; thus in the Upanishads एतस्य वा अक्षरस्य गार्गि प्रशासने सूर्याचन्द्रमसौ तिष्ठतः । or भीषाऽस्माद्वातः पवते &c.; the greatness consisting in the great powers which obey those commands.” But Rámarshi’s sense seems to be more natural. The knowledge of Brahma is called Mahâs'âsana because it comes authoritatively from the Vedas, it being the highest truth taught by them. कोऽपि—indescribable. Cf. विकारः कोप्यन्तर्जडयति च तापं च कुरुते, Utt. नित्योदित—ever existing, steady, permanent. Cf. with this sl. 70 infra.; Gîta VI. 22.
    stanza_note 42 'Sl. 42.' [u4]
      r4: Sl. 42. रम्या सा would be better for symmetry. सामन्त (सम्यगन्तः समन्तः border, frontier, तत्र भवः सामन्तः ) means a frontier king, a tributary prince. विदग्ध — a wise man. सापि राजपरिषद् v l. involves tautology since सामन्तचक्र has already occurred. उत्सिक्तः— swollen or puffed up with pride, proud. उद्रिक्त v. l. ( p p. of रिच् with उत् ) lit. exceeded, overflowed, hence haughty. निवह—a collection. स्मृतिपथमगात्—became a matter of memory i. e. history.
    stanza_note 43 'Sl. 43.' (open end) [u5]
      r5: Sl. 43. गृहे—used here in a double sense; (1) a house; (2) a square on a dice-board. तदनु बहवः—( And there are ) many after him, i. e. many who follow or depend upon him. The last two lines describe the game at dice in which the god of death and his wife (the male and female personifications of the principle of Destruction) are engaged. The world is their dice-board,

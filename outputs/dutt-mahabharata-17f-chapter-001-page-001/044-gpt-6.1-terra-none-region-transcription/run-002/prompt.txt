You are given one scanned page of a book, already divided into regions, one per printed unit of text. Transcribe each region line by line, and build the page's tree of units.

The book: Mahābhārata, Sanskrit text with M. N. Dutt's English translation. Two columns. A set of one or more Sanskrit verses is followed by one English paragraph translating the whole set. A set can run across a column or page break. Speaker lines, such as संजय उवाच and Sanjaya said, are printed with the verses and with the translation.

The first image is the whole page, with each region outlined in color and labeled with its id (r1, r2, …). After it, each region follows on its own: a line with its id, its type and its number of lines, then an image of the region cut out of the page, with everything outside the region blanked. In the gray strip at the left of each cut-out, the numbers 1, 2, … mark the lines found in the region, from the top. The strip is not part of the page.

1. Transcription
- For each region, return its lines in order, one entry per numbered line, with its number n and its text. Transcribe what is printed on that line inside the cut-out; use the whole page only for context.
- If a numbered line holds only a stray piece of a neighboring line, such as a detached vowel sign or a dot, transcribe that piece with the line it belongs to and give an empty text for the numbered line.
- Transcribe exactly as printed. Do not correct, modernize, complete or translate the text, and do not expand abbreviations. Keep the printed punctuation, digits and spelling.
- A note marker printed in the text, such as a superscript number, an asterisk or a dagger that refers to a footnote or note, is written in square brackets right after the word that carries it, as in word[1] or word[*], with the marker as printed. A footnote's own number at its start is written as printed, without brackets.
- end says how a line joins the next line of the same unit: "hyphen" if it ends with a hyphen that divides a word (keep the hyphen in the text), "join" if a word runs on to the next line without a hyphen, "line" if the line break is part of the text, as between the lines of a verse, and "space" otherwise. For a region's last line, say how it joins the unit's next text, wherever that is (on the next page, or after an interruption); use "space" if the unit ends there.
- type: the region's type. Keep the type it was given unless it is clearly wrong.
- Set uncertain=true for a region whose reading you are unsure of, and say why in note; otherwise note is null.
- Conventions for this book:
  - Transcribe Devanagari and English as printed, in Unicode NFC, with the printed digits and dandas.

2. Units
The book's unit types:
- chapter (a larger unit, made of other units; contains heading, group; may stand at the top of flow main): One chapter, with its heading.
- heading (printed as regions): A chapter heading.
- group (a larger unit, made of other units; contains speaker, verse_set, translation): A set of verses with the translation of the set, and any speaker lines printed with them.
- speaker (printed as regions): A speaker line: centered Sanskrit, such as संजय उवाच, or bold English, such as Sanjaya said.
- verse_set (a larger unit, made of other units; contains verse): One or more consecutive verses translated together.
- verse (printed as regions): One Sanskrit verse, usually two lines, with its number, such as ॥२५॥.
- translation (printed as regions): The English translation of a verse set, one paragraph.

- Every region that is not furniture is a child of exactly one unit of its own type. A unit printed as several regions, such as a paragraph interrupted by something it contains, lists all of them.
- Larger units contain smaller ones, as their types allow. List each unit's children in reading order, as region ids and unit ids.
- Furniture regions (running_header, page_number) and regions of type other belong to no unit.
- Give each unit an id (u1, u2, …), its printed number in ASCII digits if it has one (a verse numbered ॥२१॥ is "21"), and a short label or null.
- The page is processed on its own. Set open_start=true for a unit that began on an earlier page, and open_end=true for a unit that goes on on a later page. A unit open at its end is the last child of its parent, and the parent is open at its end too; likewise, a unit open at its start is the first child of a parent open at its start. A unit around the whole page, such as a chapter whose beginning and end are not on this page, is open at both ends.
- flows: the top-level units of each flow, in reading order. The book's flows:
  - main: The chapters in reading order.
Return JSON only, in the given schema.

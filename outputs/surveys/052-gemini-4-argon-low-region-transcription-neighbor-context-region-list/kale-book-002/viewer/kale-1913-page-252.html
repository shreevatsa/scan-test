stanza_note 42
r2Sl. 42. —कंदर्प—Káma, the god of love; cf. कंदर्पयामीति मदाज्जातमात्रो जगाद च । तेन कंदर्पनामानं तं चकार चतुर्भुजः ॥ किं कदर्थयसि—why do you trouble your hand. टङ्कारितैः—टङ्कारवैः v. l. टङ्क+आरव; टङ्कायतीति ( fr. टङ्क+अच् ) or टामिति शब्दं कायतीति टङ्कः an onomatopœic word. टङ्कारित however is better and has therefore been preferred. For the third line cf. supra. बाले लीलामुकुलितममी &c. Mis. sl. 40. स्निग्ध affectionate; विदग्ध cleverly cast, artful.
stanza_note 43
r3Sl. 43. उन्मीलत् &c.—त्रिवली the folds of skin above the navel of a woman regarded as a mark of beauty ( Cf. Kum. I. 39 ), डयत् &c. pairs of Chakravàka birds are usually described as haunting river-banks. क्रूराशया has two meanings (1) क्रूराः आशयाः यस्याम् in which there are dangerous aquatic animals; (2) क्रूरा आशयाः thoughts (or आशय mind) यस्याः। संसारार्णव–Samsára is the sea to which the river, in the form of a woman, leads a man.
stanza_note 44
r4Sl. 44. हतपरमार्थैः—The Indriyas which are often compared to horses, tempt a man further and further away from real bliss ( मोक्ष ).
